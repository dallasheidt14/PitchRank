Two different organizations run these platforms. The ECNL is sanctioned by US Club Soccer and fields both boys and girls competitions. MLS NEXT is operated by Major League Soccer and is boys-only. They aren't tiers of one ladder — they're parallel platforms, and most clubs pick one side rather than climbing from one to the other.

That single fact resolves most of the confusion parents run into. Below is what actually separates them: who runs each, how a club gets in, which age groups they serve, and where each season ends.

## The Short Structural Comparison

|             | ECNL                                                                               | MLS NEXT                                                  |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Operator    | Sanctioned by [US Club Soccer](https://usclubsoccer.org/ecnl/)                     | [Major League Soccer](https://www.mlssoccer.com/mlsnext/) |
| Genders     | Boys and girls                                                                     | Boys only                                                 |
| Entry route | Promotion from ECNL Regional League and Pre-ECNL, or admission from another league | Application to an expansion process                       |
| Structure   | Boys: 170+ clubs, 15 conferences. Girls: 129 clubs, 10 conferences                 | Allstate Homegrown Division and Academy Division          |
| Age groups  | Roughly U13–U18/19 in the playoff structure, with Pre-ECNL below                   | U13, U14, U15, U16, U17, U19                              |
| Postseason  | ECNL Playoffs, Champions League / Super Cup, League Cup, Conference Cup            | MLS NEXT Cup, Generation adidas Cup                       |
| Pro pathway | Showcase events; college recruiting                                                | MLS academies and MLS NEXT Pro                            |

## What Is the Difference Between ECNL and MLS NEXT in Practice?

The operator difference is the one that cascades into everything else. US Club Soccer sanctions the ECNL as, in its own words, "a top, league-based platform for both girls and boys players" — a league organization serving member clubs. MLS NEXT sits inside a professional league's development structure, which is why its calendar, its divisions, and its scouting all point toward MLS clubs.

That shapes who each platform is built around. The ECNL's stated core value is competition that "rewards clubs for their performances on the field through multiple seasons and age groups." MLS NEXT splits its membership into an Academy Division — the MLS club academies themselves — and the Allstate Homegrown Division for member clubs without an MLS affiliation.

For a family, the practical difference is usually about girls' options and about geography. The ECNL is the only one of the two with a girls side at all, so for girls the comparison is really ECNL against Girls Academy, not against MLS NEXT. For boys, both exist in most large markets, and which one a club belongs to is often a matter of club history rather than current strength.

## How Does a Club Get Into Each One?

This is the sharpest structural difference, and it is the one most parents never hear about.

**The ECNL runs a promotion pyramid.** Clubs move up through Pre-ECNL, then the ECNL Regional League, then the full ECNL, and movement is earned through results over multiple seasons. For the 2026-27 season the ECNL announced more than 60 clubs moving up or entering the pyramid — 10 new clubs into ECNL Boys (7 of them promoted directly from the Regional League), 4 new clubs into ECNL Girls (all promoted from the Regional League), plus 22 new clubs into ECNL Regional League Boys and 24 into ECNL Regional League Girls.

**MLS NEXT runs an application.** Clubs apply through an expansion process rather than earning entry through a lower division. There's no published promotion ladder feeding into it.

The consequence for a family choosing a club: an ECNL Regional League team has a defined, visible route upward, and clubs are promoted from it every year. A club outside MLS NEXT has no comparable on-field pathway into it.

## Is ECNL Higher Than MLS NEXT?

Neither platform is a division of the other, so there's no structural sense in which one sits above the other. What the data does show is that the top of the boys game and the top of the girls game are concentrated in different places.

Across the more than 58,000 teams PitchRank rates, we looked at the top 50 U15 teams in each gender. On the boys side, about three in five of those top 50 teams play in MLS NEXT, with the rest spread across ECNL, ECNL Regional League, US Youth Soccer's National League, and unaffiliated clubs. On the girls side, roughly seven in ten of the top 50 play in ECNL, with Girls Academy the next largest group.

Read that carefully, because it is a statement about _where strong teams currently sit_, not about which league develops players better. It means the honest answer to "which is higher" depends on which gender you're asking about — and, at the individual level, on the specific teams your child could actually join.

## MLS NEXT vs ECNL vs NPL: Where the Third Name Fits

Parents often see these three names together, but NPL was never a peer of the other two. The National Premier Leagues was US Club Soccer's platform below the ECNL, described by US Club Soccer as "a unified national developmental platform to various member leagues throughout the country" whose purpose was to "facilitate more meaningful games, reduce travel and provide a pathway to postseason competition."

More importantly, NPL is going away. US Club Soccer announced in July 2026 that the 2026 season was NPL's last, and that it is being replaced by the **National 1 League** for 2026-27. If you're comparing three options and one of them is NPL, you're working from a map that's about to be out of date.

## What Is After MLS NEXT?

Two different answers, depending on what "after" means.

**By age:** MLS NEXT runs through U19. After that, players move to college soccer, into MLS NEXT Pro, or out of the structured pathway entirely.

**By level:** MLS NEXT Pro sits above the academy tier. The connection is organizational, not just aspirational — the same executive, Eddie Pope, serves as Senior Vice President of Sporting Development for Major League Soccer and Senior Vice President of Competition and Operations for MLS NEXT Pro.

College is where the two platforms converge in practical terms: both run showcase events that college coaches attend, and both produce college players every year.

## What Each Postseason Actually Looks Like

The ECNL's postseason is broad. Its playoff structure draws roughly 300 qualifying teams across U13 through U18/19, alongside the Champions League and Super Cup on the boys side, the League Cup on the girls side, and Conference Cup competition.

MLS NEXT's postseason is narrower and more centralized: the MLS NEXT Cup, plus the Generation adidas Cup, which the league describes as featuring "88 teams representing 14 different countries and four continents."

Neither shape is better. A broad playoff means more teams get a meaningful postseason; a narrow one means the final rounds are concentrated. Which matters to your family depends on whether your child's team is realistically a title contender or a solid mid-table side that would benefit from extra competitive games.

## How Geography Changes the Answer

Both platforms are national, but neither is evenly distributed, and that does more to determine a family's real options than any league-level comparison.

In a large metro with several strong clubs, a boy may genuinely be able to choose between an MLS NEXT club and an ECNL club, and the comparison above is decision-relevant. In much of the country there's one realistic option within a sane drive, and the platform question resolves itself.

The ECNL's conference structure — 15 on the boys side, 10 on the girls side — means conference boundaries, not state lines, define who a team plays week to week. Two clubs an hour apart can sit in different conferences and rarely meet. Before weighing platforms, it is worth asking a club which conference a specific age group plays in and what the resulting travel radius looks like.

## What This Comparison Cannot Tell You

Everything above is structural — who runs what, who plays whom, how entry works. None of it tells you whether a particular team is a good fit for a particular child.

Three things it deliberately doesn't measure:

- **Coaching quality at your specific club.** Platform membership is a club-level fact. Your child's experience is a team-level and coach-level one.
- **Playing time.** A rotation player on a nationally ranked roster may develop more slowly than a starter one tier down.
- **Travel and cost load.** Both platforms involve significant travel. That's a family logistics question, not a soccer question.

If a club director explains their platform but can't explain their development plan for your child's age group, the platform name is doing more work in the conversation than it should.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can one club run both an ECNL team and an MLS NEXT team?

The two run parallel competition calendars, so a club's boys program is usually built around one of them. A club can still field ECNL Girls teams alongside an MLS NEXT boys program, since the ECNL has a girls side and MLS NEXT does not. Ask your club which platform each age group actually plays in.

### What age groups does MLS NEXT cover?

MLS NEXT lists U13, U14, U15, U16, U17 and U19. There is no U18 bracket, and no MLS NEXT competition below U13, so younger players in an MLS NEXT club play in other leagues until they reach the platform's youngest age group.

### How does a club get into MLS NEXT?

Through an expansion application to the league rather than by promotion from a lower division. Unlike the ECNL, MLS NEXT does not operate a published feeder tier that clubs earn their way up from, so there is no on-field route into it.

### Does the ECNL have a boys league?

Yes. The ECNL began as a girls league in 2009 and added boys competition in 2017, launching with 57 clubs. ECNL Boys now spans more than 170 clubs across 15 conferences, making it substantially larger by club count than the girls side.

### What happens to a player after MLS NEXT ends at U19?

College soccer and MLS NEXT Pro are the routes onward, and MLS NEXT Pro is organizationally linked to MLS NEXT through shared senior leadership. Where players actually land varies widely by club, so ask a club where its recent graduating players went rather than inferring it from the pathway.

### Which platform do college coaches attend?

Both platforms run showcase events, and both are attended by college coaches. Attendance varies by event, region and division, so the more useful question for a family is which specific showcases a club actually travels to and which coaches were there last year.
