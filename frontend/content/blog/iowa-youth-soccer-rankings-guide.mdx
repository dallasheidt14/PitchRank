# Iowa Youth Soccer Rankings: The Complete Parent's Guide (2026)

Two Iowa U13 teams can play a full season and see almost none of the same opponents. One plays out of the Des Moines suburbs and spends its weekends against Missouri, Nebraska and Kansas sides. The other is on the Mississippi in eastern Iowa, where the Illinois side of the Quad Cities is minutes away, and plays nearly as many Illinois teams as Iowa ones.

That's Iowa youth soccer: a state whose teams play more of their games against out-of-state teams than against each other. Your league table tells you how you did against the teams you happened to play. Rankings are how you find out what it means across the whole state.

## Why Iowa Soccer Rankings Matter

In Iowa, "who's the best team in the state" is hard to settle from results alone, because the state's strongest programs build their records against out-of-state opponents. A central Iowa team can post a strong record in Heartland competition while an eastern Iowa team posts one against Illinois clubs, and the two may never meet before State Cup.

Rankings give you three things standings can't:

1. **Cross-state context** — How does your Bettendorf or Quad City Strikers team actually compare to a Vision Soccer Academy or Sporting Iowa side with a completely different schedule?
2. **Informed club decisions** — At tryouts, rankings let you compare Vision Soccer Academy, Sporting Iowa, FC United Iowa and the eastern Iowa clubs on one scale instead of on reputation.
3. **Realistic expectations** — Winning your local flight is a starting point. ECNL, State Cup and the US Youth Soccer National Championships are where the number gets tested.

## How Iowa Soccer Rankings Actually Work

Points-based systems such as GotSoccer award points for where a team places in tournaments and league flights, which can reward volume over quality. A team that enters 15 events and wins weak brackets can outrank a team that plays a harder schedule at fewer events.

### What PitchRank Tracks for Iowa Teams

We track **every game we can find** — league play, tournaments, friendlies, showcases — across all 1,110 Iowa teams we follow.

Here's the short version of how your team's ranking is calculated:

1. **Base rating** — Every team starts neutral and moves with each game played
2. **Strength of schedule** — Beating a top ECNL side means more than beating a developmental team. Losing to a strong opponent hurts less than losing to a weak one.
3. **Recency** — Last month's games count more than games from 10 months ago
4. **Consistency** — Steady performance ranks higher than wild swings between blowout wins and bad losses

The result is a **PowerScore from 0 to 100** that lets you compare teams within the same age group at a glance.

## What Your Iowa Team's Ranking Actually Tells You

You check PitchRank and see your U14 team is ranked #8 in Iowa. What does that mean?

### State vs National Rankings

- **State rank** — Where your team stands among Iowa teams in the same age group and gender
- **National rank** — Where your team stands among all teams in that age group and gender nationwide

**Reality check:** Iowa's boards are short, and most Iowa teams play a large share of their games against out-of-state teams. A state rank tells you where you sit among the Iowa teams we've ranked; the national rank and the teams around it tell you how that compares with the Illinois, Missouri and Nebraska sides you actually play.

### Age Group Matters More Than You Think

Rankings aren't comparable across age groups. A U10 team ranked #3 in Iowa isn't in the same position as a U14 team ranked #3. Why?

- **Competition density** — U14 has 33 ranked teams; U10 has 10
- **Development stages** — Rankings are more volatile in younger age groups
- **Playing up** — Some teams compete a year up for tougher games, which changes what their record means

**Pro tip:** Always check your specific age group when looking at [Iowa soccer rankings](/rankings/ia).

### What Rankings Don't Tell You

Rankings measure competitive results. They can't measure:

- **Individual player development** — A top-ranked team might not be the right fit for your child's growth
- **Coaching quality** — Some lower-ranked teams have better developmental coaches than win-now programs
- **Team culture** — Your kid's enjoyment matters more than a number
- **College fit** — D3 coaches care about GPA and character more than PowerScore

If a club director sells you on rankings alone but can't explain their development philosophy, that's a red flag.

## How to Use Iowa Rankings When Choosing a Club

Rankings are one tool at tryout time — here's how to use them well.

### Questions to Ask Club Directors

When you're comparing Vision Soccer Academy vs Sporting Iowa, or Bettendorf vs Quad City Strikers vs North Scott:

1. **"How do your teams' rankings trend over time?"** — Upward trends suggest good coaching. Flat or declining suggests stagnation.
2. **"What's the strength of schedule for this age group?"** — Are they playing real competition or filling the calendar with easy brackets?
3. **"Which states are this team's opponents from?"** — In Iowa the answer runs from mostly Illinois teams to a mix of Missouri, Nebraska and Kansas teams, and it shapes both your weekends and how well-tested the team's ranking is.
4. **"How does the team schedule around the spring high school season?"** — Iowa plays high school soccer in the spring for boys and girls, which shapes the club calendar for older players.

### Finding the Right Competition Level

The best team for your child isn't always the highest-ranked one. Look for a team that:

- Plays opponents 10–20 ranking positions above AND below them
- Gives your child 30+ minutes per game
- Challenges without crushing confidence
- Fits your family's travel budget and schedule

### Red Flags

- **Cherry-picking opponents** — Teams that pad records against the same weak flight. Their ranking will plateau.
- **Wild ranking swings** — Could signal roster instability or inconsistent coaching
- **Ranking guarantees** — No legitimate club can promise a specific ranking

## More Out-of-State Opponents Than Iowa Ones

Here's what makes Iowa different. We've recorded **2,909 games** for ranked Iowa teams over the past year. Counting each team's own games, **only 37% were against other Iowa teams**; Illinois teams account for 26%, Missouri 10%, Nebraska 7%, Kansas 6% and Minnesota 5%.

That statewide figure hides two clear patterns:

- **Eastern Iowa plays Illinois.** Bettendorf Soccer Association plays 46% of its recorded games against Illinois teams, North Scott 54%, Quad City Strikers 44%, Southeast Soccer Academy 46% and Muscatine 40%.
- **Other big clubs play Missouri, Nebraska and Kansas teams.** Sporting Iowa plays more of its recorded games against Missouri teams (20%) than against Iowa teams (15%), with Nebraska and Kansas at 15% each. Vision Soccer Academy and FC United Iowa also play most of their games against out-of-state teams, and about 30% of UFA's Des Moines games are against Nebraska teams, the same share as against Iowa teams.

What that means for parents:

- **An Iowa team's ranking is tied closely to out-of-state opponents.** That's a strength, not a weakness — more distinct opponents means a better-supported rating.
- **Your state rank compares you with Iowa teams you may rarely play.** Two Iowa teams that never meet can still be compared, because they share opponents in regional leagues and tournaments.

## Iowa State Cup and Rankings

Iowa Soccer splits its State Cup by age because of the spring high school season: U15–U19 teams compete in the fall and U13–U14 teams in the spring. This season's fall cup was played in central Iowa, with group play October 17–18 and the championship weekend October 24–25, 2026; the spring dates for U13–U14 hadn't been posted yet. Iowa Soccer says its champion advances to represent Iowa at the US Youth Soccer National Championships.

Iowa Soccer hadn't posted 2026-27 State Cup rules when we checked. Its last posted rules, for 2024-25, opened the cup to registered teams that entered on time and met its roster and eligibility requirements. They seeded teams mainly on national-league and Midwest Conference results, then on other league and tournament play, with a blind draw where teams couldn't be seeded.

Where rankings fit in:

- **Entry** — The cup's entry rules, not your ranking, decide whether your team can play. Rankings show how your results compare with teams outside your league
- **Competition quality** — State Cup puts central and eastern Iowa programs in the same bracket, which doesn't happen often during the season
- **Exposure** — It's one of the few weekends where the state's regions are measured against each other under real stakes

If your team is ranked in the **top 10–15% in Iowa**, State Cup is where that number gets tested against the rest of the state.

## The College Recruiting Reality Check

Let's be honest about what Iowa rankings mean for recruiting.

### What Coaches Actually Look At

1. **Individual highlight video** — YOUR child, not team stats
2. **Academic eligibility** — NCAA core-course GPA requirements and each college's own admission standards filter players before rankings matter
3. **Showcase attendance** — ECNL and Girls Academy events, where coaches can see many teams in one weekend
4. **Direct contact** — Emails to coaches with video links beat high rankings

### Rankings by Division

- **Division I and II** — A strong national rank helps a team get noticed, but coaches recruit individual players
- **Division III** — Rankings barely factor in. Academics, character, and fit drive decisions.

**Iowa's reality:** with short state boards and so many out-of-state opponents, the national rank is the more useful number for recruiting conversations once your child hits U15.

### Does Iowa have ECNL and MLS NEXT teams?

### How does the Iowa State Cup work?

Because Iowa plays high school soccer in the spring, Iowa Soccer runs its State Cup for U15–U19 in the fall and for U13–U14 in the spring. The 2026 fall cup was played in central Iowa in late October, and Iowa Soccer says its champion advances to the US Youth Soccer National Championships.
