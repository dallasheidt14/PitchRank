# Oregon Youth Soccer Rankings: The Complete Parent's Guide (2026)

A U15 team from Eugene and a U15 team from Beaverton are more than 100 miles apart on I-5. The Beaverton team is much closer to its rivals in Vancouver, Washington, just across the Columbia River — and it probably plays them more often than it plays anyone from Eugene.

That's Oregon youth soccer. Most of the state's competitive clubs sit in the Portland metro, a second cluster runs down the Willamette Valley through Salem and Eugene, and the busiest cross-town rivalry for many Portland teams is with Washington. Your league record tells you how you did against the teams on your schedule. Rankings are how you find out what it means against everyone else.

## Why Oregon Soccer Rankings Matter

Oregon's schedules pull in two directions at once: down the valley between Portland, Salem and Eugene, and north across the river into Washington. A Portland club can finish a season having played Washington opponents almost as often as Oregon ones, while a Salem or Eugene club plays mostly in state. Their records aren't comparable on their face.

Rankings give you three things standings can't:

1. **Cross-region context** — How does your Westside Metros team actually compare to a Eugene Metro FC side, or a Capital FC team from Salem?
2. **Informed club decisions** — At tryouts, rankings let you compare Westside Metros, Oregon Premier, United PDX, Eastside Timbers and FC Portland on one scale instead of on reputation.
3. **Realistic expectations** — Winning your local flight is a starting point. ECNL, MLS NEXT, Girls Academy and the Oregon State Cup are where the number gets tested.

Clubs in the Portland metro account for more than half of Oregon's ranked teams, but the single biggest club is about two hours south.

## How Oregon Soccer Rankings Actually Work

Most ranking systems — GotSoccer being the biggest — award points for where a team places in tournaments and league flights, which can reward volume over quality. A team that enters 15 events and wins weak brackets can outrank a team that plays a harder schedule at fewer events.

### What PitchRank Tracks for Oregon Teams

We track **every game we can find** — league play, tournaments, friendlies, showcases — across all 1,756 Oregon teams we follow.

Here's the short version of how your team's ranking is calculated:

1. **Base rating** — Every team starts neutral and moves with each game played
2. **Strength of schedule** — Beating a top ECNL side means more than beating a developmental team. Losing to a strong opponent hurts less than losing to a weak one.
3. **Recency** — Last month's games count more than games from 10 months ago
4. **Consistency** — Steady performance ranks higher than wild swings between blowout wins and bad losses

The result is a **PowerScore between 0.0 and 1.0** that lets you compare teams within the same age group at a glance.

## What Your Oregon Team's Ranking Actually Tells You

You check PitchRank and see your U14 team is ranked #9 in Oregon. What does that mean?

### State vs National Rankings

- **State rank** — Where your team stands among Oregon teams in the same age group and gender
- **National rank** — Where your team stands among all teams in that age group and gender nationwide

**Reality check:** Oregon's ranked boards are small, so a state rank is drawn from a short list. And because so many Portland-area teams play Washington opponents every season, a team's state rank leaves out a large part of the competition it actually faces. The national rank captures all of it.

### Age Group Matters More Than You Think

Rankings aren't comparable across age groups. A U10 team ranked #5 in Oregon isn't in the same position as a U19 team ranked #5. Why?

- **Competition density** — U19 has 56 ranked teams; U10 has 16
- **Development stages** — Rankings are more volatile in younger age groups
- **Playing up** — Some teams compete a year up for tougher games, which changes what their record means

**Pro tip:** Always check your specific age group when looking at [Oregon soccer rankings](/rankings/or).

### What Rankings Don't Tell You

Rankings measure competitive results. They can't measure:

- **Individual player development** — A top-ranked team might not be the right fit for your child's growth
- **Coaching quality** — Some lower-ranked teams have better developmental coaches than win-now programs
- **Team culture** — Your kid's enjoyment matters more than a number
- **College fit** — D3 coaches care about GPA and character more than PowerScore

If a club director sells you on rankings alone but can't explain their development philosophy, that's a red flag.

## How to Use Oregon Rankings When Choosing a Club

Rankings are one tool at tryout time — here's how to use them well.

### Questions to Ask Club Directors

When you're comparing Westside Metros vs FC Portland vs Oregon Surf, or Eugene Metro FC vs Capital FC:

1. **"How do your teams' rankings trend over time?"** — Upward trends suggest good coaching. Flat or declining suggests stagnation.
2. **"What's the strength of schedule for this age group?"** — Are they playing real competition or filling the calendar with easy brackets?
3. **"How much of this age group's schedule is against Washington teams?"** — In the Portland metro, that's often a large share of the season.
4. **"How many players from this group have moved into ECNL or MLS NEXT rosters?"** — Player progression matters more than team rank.

### Finding the Right Competition Level

The best team for your child isn't always the highest-ranked one. Look for a team that:

- Plays opponents 10–20 ranking positions above AND below them
- Gives your child 30+ minutes per game
- Challenges without crushing confidence
- Fits your family's travel budget and schedule

### Red Flags

- **Cherry-picking opponents** — Teams that pad records against the same weak flight. Their ranking will plateau.
- **Wild ranking swings** — Could signal roster instability or inconsistent coaching
- **Ranking guarantees** — No legitimate club can promise a specific ranking

## Portland, the Valley and the Washington Line

Of the **4,057 games** we've recorded for ranked Oregon teams over the past year, a little over half were against other Oregon teams. **Almost a quarter were against Washington teams**, and Idaho and California opponents made up about 6% each.

That Washington share isn't spread evenly — it's mostly a Portland story:

- **Portland-metro clubs** play the most across the river. Oregon Surf SC and Soccer Chance Academy each play more than half of their recorded games against Washington teams, and Hillsboro Rush and Saints Academy play around 40–45%.
- **Willamette Valley clubs** stay much closer to home. Capital FC in Salem and Eugene Metro FC each play about two-thirds of their recorded games against Oregon teams.

What that means for parents:

- **In Portland**, the state line is a technicality. Your team's real competitive market includes Vancouver and southwest Washington, and its ranking reflects those games.
- **In Salem and Eugene**, most of the schedule is in state, so games against Portland clubs carry a lot of the weight in connecting your team to the wider picture.
- **Two Oregon teams that never meet can still be compared**, because they share opponents on both sides of the river.
- **If your team plays only inside its own area**, treat the ranking as provisional. State Cup and regional events are where it gets confirmed.

## Oregon State Cup and Rankings

The Oregon Youth Soccer Association runs the Oregon State Cup for U11 through U19. Under its 2026-27 rules, U11 teams don't advance beyond the State Cup, the U12 competition decides which teams represent Oregon at the US Youth Soccer Far West U12 Championships, and the U13–U19 competition decides who goes to the US Youth Soccer National Championships.

Where rankings fit in:

- **The draw** — State Cup brackets are set by blind draw, so a high ranking won't earn an easier bracket. Rankings do tell you what to expect from the teams you draw
- **Competition quality** — State Cup puts Portland, Salem, Eugene and Bend programs into one bracket, which doesn't happen often during the season
- **Exposure** — It's one of the few competitions where Oregon teams are measured only against each other under real stakes

If your team is ranked in the **top 10–15% in Oregon**, State Cup is where that number gets tested against the rest of the state.

## The College Recruiting Reality Check

Let's be honest about what Oregon rankings mean for recruiting.

### What Coaches Actually Look At

1. **Individual highlight video** — YOUR child, not team stats
2. **Academic eligibility** — NCAA core-course GPA requirements and each college's own admission standards filter players before rankings matter
3. **Showcase attendance** — ECNL, MLS NEXT and Girls Academy events, where coaches can see many teams in one weekend
4. **Direct contact** — Emails to coaches with video links beat high rankings

### Rankings by Division

- **Division I** — Coaches notice teams in the **top 5% nationally**
- **Division II** — Top 15–20% nationally gets attention, but individual performance matters more
- **Division III** — Rankings barely factor in. Academics, character, and fit drive decisions.

**Oregon's reality:** with Washington opponents making up almost a quarter of the recorded schedule, a state rank tells only part of the story. Once your child hits U15, the national rank is the more useful number for recruiting conversations.
