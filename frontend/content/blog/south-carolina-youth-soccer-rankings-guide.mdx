# South Carolina Youth Soccer Rankings: The Complete Parent's Guide (2026)

A U15 team from Greenville is closer to Charlotte than to Charleston, and a U15 team from Mount Pleasant can reach Savannah faster than it can reach the Upstate. So it shouldn't be a surprise where their games are: across the state line, most weekends.

That's South Carolina youth soccer. The state's competitive game runs through three areas — the Upstate around Greenville and Spartanburg, the Midlands around Columbia, and the Lowcountry around Charleston — and each one is wedged between bigger soccer markets in North Carolina and Georgia. Fewer than one in five of the games we've recorded for ranked South Carolina teams were against another South Carolina team. Your league record tells you how you did against the teams on your schedule. Rankings are how you find out what it means against everyone else.

## Why South Carolina Soccer Rankings Matter

In South Carolina, the schedule rarely answers "who's the best team in the state," because the state's top teams spend most of their season playing someone else. An ECNL side from Greenville or Columbia plays much of its conference schedule against Georgia and Florida clubs, a Charleston team may see more of North Carolina than of the Midlands, and two South Carolina teams in the same age group can go a whole season without meeting.

Rankings give you three things standings can't:

1. **Cross-state context** — How does your CESA team actually compare to a South Carolina United FC side from Columbia, or an SC Surf team from the Lowcountry?
2. **Informed club decisions** — At tryouts, rankings let you compare CESA, South Carolina United FC, SC Surf and James Island Youth SC on one scale instead of on reputation.
3. **Realistic expectations** — Winning your local flight is a starting point. ECNL, MLS NEXT, State Cup and the US Youth Soccer National Championships are where the number gets tested.

### Leagues Active in South Carolina

### Age Group Matters More Than You Think

Rankings aren't comparable across age groups. A U13 team ranked #2 in South Carolina isn't in the same position as a U14 team ranked #2. Why?

- **Competition density** — U14 has 27 ranked teams; U13 has 3
- **Development stages** — Rankings are more volatile in younger age groups
- **Playing up** — Some teams compete a year up for tougher games, which changes what their record means

### What Rankings Don't Tell You

Rankings measure competitive results. They can't measure:

- **Individual player development** — A top-ranked team might not be the right fit for your child's growth
- **Coaching quality** — Some lower-ranked teams have better developmental coaches than win-now programs
- **Team culture** — Your kid's enjoyment matters more than a number
- **College fit** — D3 coaches care about GPA and character more than PowerScore

If a club director sells you on rankings alone but can't explain their development philosophy, that's a red flag.

## How to Use South Carolina Rankings When Choosing a Club

Rankings are one tool at tryout time — here's how to use them well.

### Questions to Ask Club Directors

When you're comparing CESA vs South Carolina United FC, or SC Surf vs James Island Youth SC in Charleston:

1. **"How do your teams' rankings trend over time?"** — Upward trends suggest good coaching. Flat or declining suggests stagnation.
2. **"What's the strength of schedule for this age group?"** — Are they playing real competition or filling the calendar with easy brackets?
3. **"Where does this age group travel, and how often?"** — In South Carolina, most of the schedule is out of state, and the answer shapes your family's weekends.
4. **"How many players from this group have moved into ECNL or MLS NEXT rosters?"** — Player progression matters more than team rank.

### Finding the Right Competition Level

The best team for your child isn't always the highest-ranked one. Look for a team that:

- Plays opponents 10–20 ranking positions above AND below them
- Gives your child 30+ minutes per game
- Challenges without crushing confidence
- Fits your family's travel budget and schedule

### Red Flags

- **Cherry-picking opponents** — Teams that pad records against the same weak flight. Their ranking will plateau.
- **Wild ranking swings** — Could signal roster instability or inconsistent coaching
- **Ranking guarantees** — No legitimate club can promise a specific ranking

## Between North Carolina and Georgia

Here's the number that makes South Carolina different. Of the **2,435 games** we've recorded for ranked South Carolina teams over the past year, only **about 19% were against other South Carolina teams**. Where a club plays depends heavily on its league:

- **CESA and South Carolina United FC** play their ECNL schedules in the Southeast conference, so Georgia is their biggest opponent state — about 40% of each club's recorded games — with Florida another 12%. Only 13–15% of their recorded games are against South Carolina teams.
- **South Carolina Surf** splits its schedule: about a third of its recorded games are against Georgia teams and about a quarter against North Carolina teams, reflecting its boys in the ECNL Southeast and its girls in the Mid-Atlantic and Carolinas conferences.
- **James Island Youth SC** plays almost half of its recorded games against North Carolina teams.
- **Clemson Anderson SA** plays 39% of its recorded games against North Carolina teams and 23% against Georgia teams — and fewer than 4% against other South Carolina teams.

What that means for parents:

- **A South Carolina team's ranking is built mostly on out-of-state results.** That tends to make it more reliable, not less — more distinct opponents means a better-supported rating.
- **Two South Carolina teams that never meet can still be compared**, because they share opponents in Charlotte, Atlanta and across the Southeast.
- **If your team plays only inside its own area**, treat the ranking as provisional. State Cup and out-of-state events are where it gets confirmed.

## South Carolina State Cup and Rankings

The South Carolina Youth Soccer Association runs the South Carolina State Cup, and it has changed for 2026-27. Girls no longer have separate State Cup and Presidents Cup events — there is one unified State Cup — and both the boys and girls cups are open to teams from qualifying leagues outside the new US Youth Soccer and US Club Soccer joint pyramid, such as NAL (and DPL for girls). State Cup champions qualify for the US Youth Soccer National Championships, and girls finalists represent South Carolina at the Southeast Presidents Cup.

Seeding follows each team's league status within the joint pyramid, with teams from leagues outside it seeded after those inside, and it's set on November 1 — often before league play is finished. The semifinals and finals are played at the Florence Soccer Complex on December 19-20.

Where rankings fit in:

- **Seeding** — Your league, not your ranking, decides your seed. Rankings tell you what to expect from the teams in your bracket
- **Competition quality** — State Cup puts Upstate, Midlands and Lowcountry programs into one bracket, which doesn't happen often during a season spent mostly out of state
- **Exposure** — It's one of the few competitions where South Carolina teams are measured only against each other under real stakes

If your team is ranked in the **top 10–15% in South Carolina**, State Cup is where that number gets tested against the rest of the state.

## The College Recruiting Reality Check

Let's be honest about what South Carolina rankings mean for recruiting.

### What Coaches Actually Look At

1. **Individual highlight video** — YOUR child, not team stats
2. **Academic eligibility** — NCAA core-course GPA requirements and each college's own admission standards filter players before rankings matter
3. **Showcase attendance** — ECNL, MLS NEXT and Girls Academy events, where coaches can see many teams in one weekend
4. **Direct contact** — Emails to coaches with video links beat high rankings

### Rankings by Division

- **Division I** — Coaches notice teams in the **top 5% nationally**
- **Division II** — Top 15–20% nationally gets attention, but individual performance matters more
- **Division III** — Rankings barely factor in. Academics, character, and fit drive decisions.

**South Carolina's reality:** with roughly four of every five recorded games played against out-of-state opponents, the state's top programs are measured against Southeast competition every season. Once your child hits U15, the national rank is the more useful number for recruiting conversations.
