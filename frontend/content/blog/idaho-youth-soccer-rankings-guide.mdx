# Idaho Youth Soccer Rankings: The Complete Parent's Guide (2026)

A U14 team in Coeur d'Alene and a U14 team in Pocatello are separated by about 550 miles of highway. They will almost certainly never play each other. They may both be excellent. There is no scoreboard anywhere that puts them on the same line.

That's the Idaho problem. The state is long, mountainous, and split into pockets of soccer that operate almost independently — the Treasure Valley around Boise, North Idaho up near the Washington border, the Magic Valley in Twin Falls, and Eastern Idaho from Pocatello up through Idaho Falls and Rexburg. Your team's league record tells you how you did against your neighbours. It tells you nothing about the rest of the state.

## Why Idaho Soccer Rankings Matter

In a dense state, your schedule does the ranking work for you — play enough teams and the standings sort themselves out. Idaho doesn't work that way. A Sandpoint team's season and a Pocatello team's season barely overlap in opponents, so their records aren't comparable on their face.

Rankings give you three things standings can't:

1. **Cross-region context** — How does your Treasure Valley team actually compare to a Sting SC roster out of Coeur d'Alene, or a CGA Academy side from Pocatello?
2. **Informed club decisions** — At tryouts, rankings put Idaho Rush, Boise Timbers | Thorns FC, Idaho Inferno, North Boise FC and Idaho Surf on one scale instead of on reputation.
3. **Realistic expectations** — Winning your local flight is a starting point. Idaho State Cup, the Far West Regionals and ECNL play are where the number is tested.

We track **922 Idaho teams** that have played in the past year, across 57 organizations. Of those, **595 have played enough games to carry a published ranking** — that's the number behind every table on this page.

### Geographic Soccer Regions

- **Treasure Valley (Boise / Meridian / Nampa)** — The centre of gravity, and it isn't close. Idaho Rush (51 ranked teams), Boise Timbers | Thorns FC (35), Idaho Inferno (34), North Boise FC (32), Idaho Surf SC (22) and Idaho Juniors FC all operate here. This is where Idaho's ECNL and ECNL Regional League teams are based, and where the deepest in-state competition happens week to week.

This region sits closer to Spokane than to Boise, and its competitive life runs across the Washington line as much as down the state.
- **Southeast Idaho (Pocatello)** — PVSC United (28 ranked teams) and CGA Academy SC are based in Pocatello, with CGA also running training centres in Idaho Falls and Teton Valley.
- **Eastern Idaho (Idaho Falls / Rexburg / Teton Valley)** — Bonneville Youth Soccer League (46 ranked teams) organizes competitive play in the Idaho Falls area, with Upper Valley Soccer Foundation covering Rexburg and Teton FC in the Teton Valley.
- **Magic Valley (Twin Falls)** — TF Rapids SC is the largest and longest-established competitive club in south-central Idaho.
- **Wood River Valley** — Sun Valley Soccer Club serves the Ketchum and Hailey corridor.
- **Multi-site** — ALBION SC Idaho (23 ranked teams) runs tryout hubs in Meridian, Mountain Home and Sandpoint, which puts it in two of the state's regions at once.

## What Your Idaho Team's Ranking Actually Tells You

You check PitchRank and see your U13 team is ranked #18 in Idaho. What does that mean?

### State vs National Rankings

- **State rank** — Where your team stands among Idaho teams in the same age group and gender
- **National rank** — Where your team stands among all teams in that age group and gender nationwide

**Reality check:** with 595 ranked teams statewide, Idaho's boards are small. A top-10 state finish is a real achievement, but the pool it comes from is a fraction of what a California or Texas team competes in. National rank is the more honest number for families thinking about college soccer, and Idaho teams that travel tend to have far better-supported rankings than teams that don't.

### Age Group Matters More Than You Think

Rankings aren't comparable across age groups. A U11 team ranked #15 in Idaho isn't in the same position as a U17 team ranked #15. Why?

- **Competition density** — U11 has 111 ranked teams; U17 has 38
- **Development stages** — Rankings are more volatile in younger age groups
- **Playing up** — Teams in thin regions often play a year up simply to find games, which changes what their record means

**Pro tip:** Always check your specific age group when looking at [Idaho soccer rankings](/rankings/id).

### What Rankings Don't Tell You

Rankings measure competitive results. They can't measure:

- **Individual player development** — A top-ranked team might not be the right fit for your child's growth
- **Coaching quality** — Some lower-ranked teams have better developmental coaches than win-now programs
- **Team culture** — Your kid's enjoyment matters more than a number
- **College fit** — D3 coaches care about GPA and character more than PowerScore

If a club director sells you on rankings alone but can't explain their development philosophy, that's a red flag.

## How to Use Idaho Rankings When Choosing a Club

In Idaho, the club decision is usually also a travel decision. Here's how to weigh rankings against that.

### Questions to Ask Club Directors

When you're comparing Idaho Rush vs Boise Timbers | Thorns FC vs Idaho Inferno vs North Boise FC vs Idaho Surf:

1. **"How many games will we play outside the Treasure Valley?"** — This is the single most important question in Idaho. It determines both development and how meaningful your ranking will be.
2. **"How do your teams' rankings trend over time?"** — Upward trends suggest good coaching. Flat or declining suggests stagnation.
3. **"What's the strength of schedule for this age group?"** — Are they playing real competition or repeating the same local opponents?
4. **"What's the realistic travel budget?"** — Idaho's out-of-state competition is in Utah, Washington, Oregon and Nevada. Ask for a number before you commit.

### Finding the Right Competition Level

The best team for your child isn't always the highest-ranked one. Look for a team that:

- Plays opponents 10–20 ranking positions above AND below them
- Gives your child 30+ minutes per game
- Challenges without crushing confidence
- Fits your family's travel budget and schedule

**Idaho's reality:** outside the Treasure Valley, the number of teams at your child's level within an hour's drive may be in the single digits. That makes the choice less about optimizing and more about finding a program that will commit to travelling for real games.

### Red Flags

- **A schedule that never leaves the region** — In Idaho this is common, and it caps both development and ranking reliability
- **Wild ranking swings** — Could signal roster instability or inconsistent coaching
- **Ranking guarantees** — No legitimate club can promise a specific ranking

## The Treasure Valley vs Everywhere Else Dynamic

Idaho's competitive soccer is more concentrated than almost any state's. Roughly a third of the state's ranked teams sit in the Boise–Meridian–Nampa corridor, and every ECNL and ECNL Regional League team in Idaho is based there.

What that means for parents:

- **In the Treasure Valley**, you have genuine week-to-week competition and a national-league pathway without leaving the metro. Idaho Rush and Boise Timbers | Thorns FC are the two clubs holding ECNL rosters, and the gap between their top teams and the rest of the valley shows up clearly in the rankings.
- **In North Idaho**, Coeur d'Alene and Sandpoint teams orient toward Spokane. Their opponents are often out of state, which actually makes their rankings better-connected to the national picture than a team playing only inside Idaho.
- **In Eastern and Southeast Idaho**, Pocatello, Idaho Falls and Rexburg run a real ecosystem, but it's separated from Boise by several hours. These teams are frequently underrated early in a season simply because they haven't yet played anyone the algorithm has a strong read on.
- **Cross-region play** is the cleanest measure of strength. When your team crosses the state or the state line, the ranking sharpens fast.

If your team plays only inside its own region, treat the ranking as provisional until State Cup.

## Idaho State Cup and Rankings

The Idaho State Cup, run by the Idaho Youth Soccer Association, is the one event that pulls every region into a single bracket. Rankings intersect with it directly:

- **Seeding** — Higher-ranked teams generally earn better draws
- **Competition quality** — It's the clearest read on the state all year, because North Idaho, the Treasure Valley and Eastern Idaho finally meet
- **Advancement** — State Cup is the qualifying route to the President's Cup and the Far West Regionals, where Idaho teams are measured against the rest of the region

If your team is ranked in the **top 10–15% in Idaho**, State Cup is where that number gets confirmed or corrected.

## The College Recruiting Reality Check

Let's be honest about what Idaho rankings mean for recruiting.

### What Coaches Actually Look At

1. **Individual highlight video** — YOUR child, not team stats
2. **Academic eligibility** — GPA and test scores filter players before rankings matter
3. **Showcase attendance** — ECNL and ECNL RL events, plus the Utah, Washington and Nevada showcase circuits Idaho clubs travel to
4. **Direct contact** — Emails to coaches with video links beat high rankings

### Rankings by Division

- **Division I** — Coaches notice teams in the **top 5% nationally**
- **Division II** — Top 15–20% nationally gets attention, but individual performance matters more
- **Division III** — Rankings barely factor in. Academics, character, and fit drive decisions.

**Idaho's reality:** the in-state college soccer market is small, and most recruiting happens at out-of-state events. For an Idaho family, showcase travel isn't a luxury add-on — it's the primary mechanism by which a coach sees your child at all. Rankings help you decide which events are worth the drive.

### How do North Idaho and Treasure Valley clubs compare in rankings?

Idaho's soccer regions are hundreds of miles apart, so a team that plays only locally faces the same small set of opponents repeatedly. The algorithm can rate a team confidently only through the opponents it shares with the rest of the country, so a schedule that crosses regions or state lines produces a far more reliable number than one that never leaves home.
