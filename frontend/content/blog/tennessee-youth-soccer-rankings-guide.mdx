# Tennessee Youth Soccer Rankings: The Complete Parent's Guide (2026)

A U14 team from Collierville and a U14 team from Knoxville are more than 350 miles apart, most of it on I-40. In a typical season they might share a single tournament weekend, or never meet at all. The Collierville side will see plenty of teams from across the Mississippi line; the Knoxville side is more likely to be driving to Georgia.

That's Tennessee youth soccer. The state's competitive game runs through four metros — Nashville, Memphis, Knoxville and Chattanooga — spread across all three of Tennessee's Grand Divisions, and each one faces a different set of neighbours. Your league record tells you how you did against the teams near you. Rankings are how you find out what it means everywhere else.

## Why Tennessee Soccer Rankings Matter

In most states, "who's the best team in the state" is a question the schedule eventually answers. In Tennessee, the schedule mostly points out of state. Memphis clubs play into Mississippi and Arkansas, Chattanooga and Knoxville clubs play into Georgia, and Nashville's biggest programs travel all over the Southeast and Midwest. Two Tennessee teams can post identical records against almost entirely different opponents.

Rankings give you three things standings can't:

1. **Cross-state context** — How does your Tennessee SC team actually compare to a Germantown Legends side out of Memphis, or an FC Alliance team from Knoxville?
2. **Informed club decisions** — At tryouts, rankings let you compare Tennessee SC, Lobos Rush, Tennessee United, Nashville United and One Knox on one scale instead of on reputation.
3. **Realistic expectations** — Winning your local flight is a starting point. ECNL, MLS NEXT, State Cup and the US Youth Soccer National Championships are where the number gets tested.

### Leagues Active in Tennessee

Tennessee has the full national-league ladder, and it's spread across the state rather than concentrated in one city. Here's where each league sits for the 2026-27 season:

- **ECNL** — Tennessee SC and FC Alliance field boys and girls teams. Germantown Legends, Chattanooga Red Wolves SC, Lobos Rush and Tennessee United SC field boys teams, with Tennessee United's boys newly promoted for 2026-27.
- **ECNL Regional League** — Boys and girls teams from Tennessee SC, FC Alliance, Germantown Legends and Lobos Rush; girls teams from Tennessee United SC and Chattanooga Red Wolves SC.
- **MLS NEXT** — Nashville SC, Nashville United Soccer Academy and Chattanooga FC Academy in the Homegrown Division, and Kings Hammer and One Knox in the Academy Division.
- **Girls Academy (GA)** — Nashville United Soccer Academy and One Knox, with Kings Hammer and Chattanooga FC Academy in GA's Aspire league.
- **National 1 League** — The league US Club Soccer and US Youth Soccer formed for 2026-27 by bringing together the National Premier Leagues (NPL) and US Youth Soccer's National League. Nashville United Soccer Academy is among its Tennessee clubs.

Plus state-level competition through the [Tennessee State Soccer Association](https://www.tnsoccer.org/), the US Youth Soccer state association, founded in 1976, which runs the Tennessee State Cup.

1. **Base rating** — Every team starts neutral and moves with each game played

## What Your Tennessee Team's Ranking Actually Tells You

You check PitchRank and see your U15 team is ranked #12 in Tennessee. What does that mean?

### State vs National Rankings

- **State rank** — Where your team stands among Tennessee teams in the same age group and gender
- **National rank** — Where your team stands among all teams in that age group and gender nationwide

**Reality check:** Tennessee's ranked boards are small, so a state rank is drawn from a short list — at U10 there are six ranked teams across boys and girls combined. Because Tennessee teams play so much of their schedule out of state, their results are well connected to the national picture, and the national rank is often the more informative number.

### Age Group Matters More Than You Think

Rankings aren't comparable across age groups. A U11 team ranked #5 in Tennessee isn't in the same position as a U14 team ranked #5. Why?

- **Competition density** — U14 has 47 ranked teams; U11 has 17
- **Development stages** — Rankings are more volatile in younger age groups
- **Playing up** — Some teams compete a year up for tougher games, which changes what their record means

**Pro tip:** Always check your specific age group when looking at [Tennessee soccer rankings](/rankings/tn).

### What Rankings Don't Tell You

Rankings measure competitive results. They can't measure:

- **Individual player development** — A top-ranked team might not be the right fit for your child's growth
- **Coaching quality** — Some lower-ranked teams have better developmental coaches than win-now programs
- **Team culture** — Your kid's enjoyment matters more than a number
- **College fit** — D3 coaches care about GPA and character more than PowerScore

If a club director sells you on rankings alone but can't explain their development philosophy, that's a red flag.

## How to Use Tennessee Rankings When Choosing a Club

Rankings are one tool at tryout time — here's how to use them well.

### Questions to Ask Club Directors

When you're comparing Tennessee SC vs Tennessee United vs Nashville United, or Lobos Rush vs Germantown Legends:

1. **"How do your teams' rankings trend over time?"** — Upward trends suggest good coaching. Flat or declining suggests stagnation.
2. **"What's the strength of schedule for this age group?"** — Are they playing real competition or filling the calendar with easy brackets?
3. **"Where does this age group travel, and how often?"** — In Tennessee the answer shapes both your family's weekends and how reliable the team's ranking is.
4. **"How many players from this group have moved into ECNL or MLS NEXT rosters?"** — Player progression matters more than team rank.

### Finding the Right Competition Level

The best team for your child isn't always the highest-ranked one. Look for a team that:

- Plays opponents 10–20 ranking positions above AND below them
- Gives your child 30+ minutes per game
- Challenges without crushing confidence
- Fits your family's travel budget and schedule

### Red Flags

- **Cherry-picking opponents** — Teams that pad records against the same weak flight. Their ranking will plateau.
- **Wild ranking swings** — Could signal roster instability or inconsistent coaching
- **Ranking guarantees** — No legitimate club can promise a specific ranking

## Tennessee Plays Across State Lines

Here's the number that makes Tennessee different. Of the **3,893 games** we've recorded for ranked Tennessee teams over the past year, only **about a third were against other Tennessee teams**. Georgia opponents account for 13%, Mississippi for 10%, and Ohio, North Carolina, Indiana and Kentucky together for roughly 15%.

Each metro leans a different way:

- **Memphis** clubs play into the Mid-South. Lobos Rush, Midsouth Futbol and Germantown Legends each play roughly a fifth to a quarter of their recorded games against Mississippi teams, and Midsouth Futbol another 18% against Arkansas. The Memphis metro itself crosses the state line, so many of those games are a short drive.
- **Chattanooga and Knoxville** clubs play into Georgia. Chattanooga FC Academy plays 43% of its recorded games against Georgia teams, and One Knox 31%.
- **Nashville's** national-league programs range furthest. Nashville United Soccer Academy plays only about a fifth of its recorded games in state, and more than a fifth against Georgia teams alone.

What that means for parents:

- **A Tennessee team's schedule is rarely Tennessee-only**, so its ranking is tied to opponents from half a dozen states. That tends to make the number more reliable, not less — more distinct opponents means a better-supported rating.
- **Two Tennessee teams that never play each other can still be compared**, because they share opponents in Georgia, Mississippi and the Midwest.
- **If your team plays only inside its own metro**, treat the ranking as provisional. State Cup and out-of-state events are where it gets confirmed.

## Tennessee State Cup and Rankings

The Tennessee State Soccer Association runs a fall State Cup for boys U15–U19 and a spring State Cup for girls U12–U19 and boys U12–U14. Only the top eight teams in the final Tennessee State League standings qualify. In the U13–U19 age groups, champions of the top National Championship Series division advance directly to the US Youth Soccer National Championships, while Presidents Cup division champions go first to a national qualifier.

Where rankings fit in:

- **Qualification** — Your final Tennessee State League standing, not your ranking, decides whether your team plays State Cup. Rankings show how that league finish compares with teams outside your division
- **Competition quality** — State Cup pulls Memphis, Nashville, Knoxville and Chattanooga programs into one bracket, which doesn't happen often during the season
- **Exposure** — It's one of the few weekends where the state's metros are measured against each other under real stakes

If your team is ranked in the **top 10–15% in Tennessee** and qualifies through the State League, State Cup is where that number gets tested against the rest of the state.

## The College Recruiting Reality Check

Let's be honest about what Tennessee rankings mean for recruiting.

### What Coaches Actually Look At

1. **Individual highlight video** — YOUR child, not team stats
2. **Academic eligibility** — NCAA core-course GPA requirements and each college's own admission standards filter players before rankings matter
3. **Showcase attendance** — ECNL, MLS NEXT and Girls Academy events, where coaches can see many teams in one weekend
4. **Direct contact** — Emails to coaches with video links beat high rankings

### Rankings by Division

- **Division I** — Coaches notice teams in the **top 5% nationally**
- **Division II** — Top 15–20% nationally gets attention, but individual performance matters more
- **Division III** — Rankings barely factor in. Academics, character, and fit drive decisions.

**Tennessee's reality:** because Tennessee teams already play most of their recorded games out of state, the state's top programs are measured against Southeast and Midwest competition every season. Once your child hits U15, the national rank is the more useful number for recruiting conversations.
